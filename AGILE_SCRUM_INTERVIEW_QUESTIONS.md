# Agile and Scrum interview questions and answers

This guide separates Agile principles, the Scrum framework, and optional team practices. The Scrum answers follow the current official Scrum Guide, published in November 2020. The Kanban comparison follows the May 2025 Kanban Guide.

## Study checklist

- [ ] Describe one sprint from planning through retrospective.
- [ ] Explain what changed after feedback from users or stakeholders.
- [ ] Separate Scrum requirements from local practices such as story points.

## Agile principles

Sources: [Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/), [Agile principles](https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html).

### 1. What does Agile mean in software development?

It describes values and principles that favor frequent delivery, customer collaboration, adapting to change, and sustainable technical work. It does not prescribe one process or meeting schedule.

### 2. Does the Agile Manifesto say documentation is unnecessary?

No. It values working software more than comprehensive documentation while acknowledging value in both. Write the documentation needed to build, operate, and understand the product.

### 3. How can a team respond to changing requirements without losing focus?

Keep the current goal visible, deliver in small increments, and review new evidence with the product decision-maker. Reorder future work when priorities change and renegotiate current scope when needed.

### 4. What is iterative versus incremental delivery?

Iteration revisits and improves a solution using feedback. Incremental delivery adds usable capability in steps. A team can do both: release a small feature, learn from its use, and improve it in a later increment.

### 5. Why does technical quality matter to agility?

Automated checks, clear design, and manageable debt make later changes safer. If each change risks breaking old behavior, responding to feedback becomes slower and more expensive.

## Scrum framework and accountabilities

Source: [The 2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html).

### 6. What is Scrum?

A lightweight framework for complex work. A Scrum Team delivers an Increment during a Sprint, inspects the result with stakeholders, and adapts its next steps.

### 7. What are Scrum's three pillars?

Transparency, inspection, and adaptation. The team makes work and results visible, checks them against goals, and changes the plan or approach based on what it learns.

### 8. What are the five Scrum values?

Commitment, focus, openness, respect, and courage. They guide how the team works together and handles difficult information.

### 9. Who belongs to a Scrum Team?

One Product Owner, one Scrum Master, and Developers. The team is cross-functional and self-managing, with no separate subteams or hierarchy inside the Scrum Team.

### 10. What is the Product Owner accountable for?

Maximizing product value and effective Product Backlog management, including the Product Goal and ordering backlog items. The Product Owner can delegate work but remains accountable for the result.

### 11. What is the Scrum Master accountable for?

Establishing Scrum as described in the guide and improving the Scrum Team's effectiveness. That includes coaching, helping remove impediments, and helping the organization work with the team.

### 12. What are Developers accountable for?

Creating the Sprint Backlog, meeting the Definition of Done, adapting their plan toward the Sprint Goal, and holding one another accountable. In Scrum, "Developers" includes everyone who builds the usable Increment, regardless of job title.

### 13. Is a Scrum Master the team's project manager?

The accountabilities are different. The Scrum Master helps the team use Scrum and improve its effectiveness; the Developers manage their work, and the Product Owner orders the Product Backlog. An organization may have project managers, but that title is not a Scrum accountability.

## Events and planning

Source: [The 2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html).

### 14. What is a Sprint?

A fixed-length event of one month or less during which the team creates value. The next Sprint begins immediately after the previous one. Planning, Daily Scrums, Review, and Retrospective happen within it.

### 15. Can the Sprint scope change?

Yes. The team can clarify and renegotiate scope with the Product Owner as it learns. Changes must not endanger the Sprint Goal, and quality must not decrease.

### 16. Who can cancel a Sprint?

Only the Product Owner, when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete. The team then inspects completed work and replans rather than treating cancellation as an ordinary way to handle a small delay.

### 17. What questions does Sprint Planning answer?

Why the Sprint is valuable, what can be Done, and how the selected work will be done. The team defines a Sprint Goal; Developers select items with the Product Owner and plan the work.

### 18. What is the Daily Scrum for?

Developers inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan. It is a 15-minute event, not a status report to the Scrum Master. Developers can choose its format.

### 19. What happens at a Sprint Review?

The Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the Sprint outcome and discuss changes in the environment. They adapt the Product Backlog as needed. It is a working session, not merely a demonstration or approval gate.

### 20. What happens at a Sprint Retrospective?

The Scrum Team inspects how it worked and plans changes that improve quality and effectiveness. It can add the most useful improvement to the next Sprint Backlog.

### 21. What are the event timeboxes for a one-month Sprint?

Sprint Planning is at most eight hours, the Daily Scrum has a 15-minute timebox, Sprint Review is at most four hours, and Sprint Retrospective is at most three hours. Shorter Sprints normally use shorter planning, review, and retrospective sessions.

### 22. Is backlog refinement a formal Scrum event?

No. It is an ongoing activity that breaks down and clarifies Product Backlog items. Teams choose when and how to do it.

## Artifacts, goals, and quality

Source: [The 2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html).

### 23. What are the three Scrum artifacts and their commitments?

The Product Backlog has the Product Goal. The Sprint Backlog has the Sprint Goal. The Increment has the Definition of Done. The commitments give each artifact a clear purpose for inspection.

### 24. What is the Product Backlog?

An emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product. It is the single source of work for the Scrum Team and changes as the team learns.

### 25. What is the Sprint Backlog?

The Sprint Goal, selected Product Backlog items, and the Developers' plan to deliver them. Developers update it through the Sprint as more becomes known.

### 26. What is the Definition of Done?

A shared description of the quality measures an Increment must meet. Work that does not meet it cannot count as part of the Increment. An organizational standard, if one exists, sets a minimum.

### 27. Is "Done" the same as "deployed"?

Not necessarily. A Done Increment is usable and meets the Definition of Done. It may be released before the Sprint ends; the Sprint Review is not a release gate. Deployment timing depends on the product's release process.

### 28. What happens to an item that is unfinished at the end of a Sprint?

It does not count as part of the Increment. The Product Owner and team revisit it in the Product Backlog and decide whether and when to select it again.

### 29. What is the difference between a Product Goal and a Sprint Goal?

The Product Goal is a longer-term objective for the product. The Sprint Goal explains why the current Sprint matters and gives the team a shared focus for its short-term work.

### 30. Are user stories, story points, and velocity required by Scrum?

No. They are optional ways to describe or forecast work. The Scrum Guide requires its accountabilities, events, artifacts, and commitments, but does not mandate a story template, point scale, or velocity metric.

### 31. How should estimates be used?

Use estimates to expose uncertainty and support a forecast, not as a promise or a measure of individual productivity. Past delivery, capacity, risk, and the Definition of Done all affect what a team can select.

## Agile teamwork and Kanban

Sources: [Agile principles](https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html), [The 2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html), [The Kanban Guide, May 2025](https://kanbanguides.org/the-kanban-guide/).

### 32. How does Kanban differ from Scrum?

Scrum organizes work around Sprints, specific accountabilities, events, and artifacts. Kanban defines and visualizes a workflow, manages items as they flow, and improves that workflow. A team can use Kanban practices alongside Scrum.

### 33. What is a work-in-progress limit?

A control on how much work has started but not finished. It helps a team finish items before starting more and makes bottlenecks visible. In Kanban, the control belongs in the Definition of Workflow.

### 34. What are useful Kanban flow metrics?

The Kanban Guide names work in progress, throughput, work item age, and cycle time. Use them to find delays and forecast delivery rather than to rank people.

### 35. What is the difference between cycle time and lead time?

Cycle time measures from when an item starts to when it finishes. Lead time often measures from request to delivery, though teams should define their own boundaries clearly before comparing the two.

### 36. How do you handle a blocked task?

Make the blocker visible, identify who can resolve it, and coordinate the next action. Replan with the team if it threatens the Sprint Goal or makes a flow forecast unreliable.

## Practical interview scenarios

### 37. A stakeholder asks for urgent work halfway through a Sprint. What do you do?

Discuss urgency and impact with the Product Owner and Developers. If the Sprint Goal remains sound, renegotiate scope while protecting quality. If the goal is obsolete, only the Product Owner can cancel the Sprint.

### 38. The team finishes every ticket but users dislike the result. What should change?

Inspect the product outcome with users and stakeholders, clarify the Product Goal, and adjust the backlog. Ticket completion is evidence of output, not proof of value.

### 39. Daily Scrum has become a manager status meeting. How do you improve it?

Bring the discussion back to progress toward the Sprint Goal and the Developers' plan for the next day. Move detailed problem-solving into follow-up conversations with the people involved.

### 40. The team repeatedly carries unfinished work into the next Sprint. What do you inspect?

Check item size, dependencies, capacity, interruptions, and whether the Definition of Done is understood. Select less work or split items into usable increments; use the Retrospective to agree on one concrete change.
